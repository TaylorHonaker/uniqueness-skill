# UNIQUENESS — The Sheahan's Wall Rubric

A scoring system for figuring out what you should be known for, and how far you
currently are from being known for it.

## 1. THE WALL

Two groups exist in every market. On one side, the unknown. On the other, the
known and trusted. Between them sits an invisible wall.

The unknown try to breach it by copying the known, who are visibly doing many
things at once. That is a misread of sequence. The known earned the right to
diversify **after** they broke through. Copying post-breach behavior while you
are still pre-breach is the single most common cause of permanent obscurity.

**Diluted focus produces diluted results.** Light through a window warms
nothing. The same light through a lens burns a hole.

The breach is always the same mechanism: become known for ONE thing.

This matters most when marketing is not working. Broad positioning does not
fail loudly. It fails quietly, by producing activity that never accumulates.
You post, you pitch, you run ads, and none of it compounds, because each piece
points somewhere slightly different and nothing ever stacks into a reputation.

## 2. THE FOUR COMPONENTS

Every run produces exactly four lines. No more.

| # | Component | Constraint | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PROBLEM** | One word | What problem do you solve? |
| 2 | **AUDIENCE** | One phrase, 6 words max | Who do you solve it for? |
| 3 | **MESSAGE** | One sentence | How do you solve it? |
| 4 | **REVENUE** | One stream | What is the single most important way you get paid? |

The word limits are the mechanism, not a formatting preference. A two-word
problem is a two-hole lens. Enforce them literally.

---

## 3. THE RUBRIC (100 POINTS)

Score each of the four components on five dimensions. 0 to 5 each. Twenty
cells, one hundred points.

### The five dimensions

**S — SPECIFICITY.** Is it a point or a window?
- **0** A category. "Business," "growth," "marketing," "leadership."
- **3** A named niche that still contains obvious sub-niches.
- **5** A point. Cannot be subdivided further without becoming a different business.

**O — OWNABILITY.** Could a direct competitor say this identical thing?
- **0** Fifty firms could say it word for word.
- **3** Three or four could, with slight strain.
- **5** Saying it truthfully requires an asset only you have. A background, a dataset, a method, a credential, a scar.

**D — DURABILITY.** Does it survive repetition, delegation, and 24 months?
- **0** Tied to a tool, platform, or trend that expires.
- **3** Survives a year, but only you can deliver it.
- **5** A hire could deliver it, it will still be true in two years, and it gets stronger every time it is repeated.

**V — SERVICE.** Is it framed around what changes for them? (The Winget test.)
- **0** Describes your capability, credentials, or tech stack.
- **3** Mixed. Mentions their outcome but leads with your method.
- **5** Names their changed state first. Your method is secondary or invisible.

**E — EVIDENCE.** Do you have receipts?
- **0** Aspiration. Nothing delivered yet.
- **3** One or two delivered instances, no measured result.
- **5** Three or more delivered instances with a number attached.

### The grid

Score every cell. Show the grid. No blanks.

```
                 S    O    D    V    E   | Subtotal
PROBLEM         [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  |   /25
AUDIENCE        [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  |   /25
MESSAGE         [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  |   /25
REVENUE         [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  |   /25
                                          -----------

```

## 4. HARD GATES (RUN BEFORE SCORING)

Any gate failure caps the run. Report the failure. Do not proceed to a passing
verdict.

| Gate | Test | On failure |
|---|---|---|
| **G1 — Word count** | Problem is 1 word. Audience is 6 words or fewer. Message is 1 sentence. | REJECT. Rewrite before scoring. |
| **G2 — Conjunction** | Does the message join two distinct value propositions with "and," "plus," a slash, or a comma series? | REJECT. That is two businesses. Pick one. |
| **G3 — Substitution** | Name three real competitors. Substitute each into your message. Does it stay true? | True for 2 or more: REJECT as commodity. Cap Ownability at 1. |
| **G4 — Revenue concentration** | Does the one named stream represent 60% or more of current or intended revenue? | Under 60%: DILUTED. Cap the total at 59. |
| **G5 — Service** | Does the message lead with their changed state, or with your capability? | Leads with capability: REJECT. Cap the whole Service column at 2. |
| **G6 — Recall** | Ask five real people who know you to name your one word from memory. | Fewer than 3 correct: the Wall is intact regardless of score. Report as UNBREACHED. |

G6 needs real people and cannot be simulated. If it has not been run, mark the
verdict **PROVISIONAL** and make running it this week's first action.

---

## 5. DILUTION INDEX

The Wall is breached by subtraction, so measure the subtraction debt.

```
DI = (active offers) x (distinct audiences served) x (revenue streams above 15%)
```

| DI | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **1 to 3** | Focused. A lens. | Defend it. |
| **4 to 8** | Drifting. | Delete or park one factor this quarter. |
| **9 to 20** | A window. Nothing compounds. | Deletion pass required before any new launch. |
| **20+** | Fog. | Freeze all new offers. Subtract before you build. |

Report DI next to the Wall Score every time. A 90-point score sitting on a DI
of 14 is a paper score.

---

## 6. VERDICT BANDS

| Score | Verdict | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|---|
| **90 to 100** | THROUGH | Known for one thing. You have earned the right to diversify deliberately. | Defend and distribute. |
| **75 to 89** | BREACHING | One component lags. | Narrow the weakest component. Re-score in 30 days. |
| **60 to 74** | SCRATCHING | Recognizable but not memorable. | Delete one offer. Re-score. |
| **40 to 59** | DILUTED | Working hard, invisible anyway. | No launches or rebrands until re-scored above 60. |
| **0 to 39** | INVISIBLE | A window, not a lens. | Full four-part rewrite from scratch. |

**The amplification rule:** anything under 60 blocks new campaigns, launches,
and content pushes. Positioning that cannot be scored cannot be amplified.
Buying reach for a message nobody will remember is the most expensive mistake
in marketing, and it looks like progress the entire time it is happening.

---

## 7. THE ONE NARROWING MOVE

Every run ends with exactly one move. Not a list.

Find the lowest-scoring **cell** in the grid, not the lowest component. That
cell is the constraint. Prescribe the single subtraction, deletion, or rewrite
that raises it. State it as an action with an owner and a date.

If two cells tie, break the tie in this order: **PROBLEM > AUDIENCE > MESSAGE >
REVENUE.** The problem word governs everything downstream of it.

## 8. OUTPUT FORMAT — THE UNIQUENESS CARD

Always produce this. One page. No preamble.

```
UNIQUENESS CARD — [Subject] — [Date]

PROBLEM   : [one word]
AUDIENCE  : [one phrase]
MESSAGE   : [one sentence]
REVENUE   : [one stream]

GATES     : G1 [P/F]  G2 [P/F]  G3 [P/F]  G4 [P/F]  G5 [P/F]  G6 [P/F/untested]

GRID
                 S    O    D    V    E   | Subtotal
PROBLEM          -    -    -    -    -   |   /25
AUDIENCE         -    -    -    -    -   |   /25
MESSAGE          -    -    -    -    -   |   /25
REVENUE          -    -    -    -    -   |   /25

WALL SCORE       : XX/100
DILUTION INDEX   : X
VERDICT          : [BAND]

THE CONSTRAINT   : [lowest cell, named]
THE ONE MOVE     : [single action, owner, date]
NOT DOING        : [2 to 3 things explicitly parked to protect the focus]

THIS WEEK        : [something executable in under 30 minutes today]
```

The NOT DOING line is mandatory. A narrowing exercise that adds work has
failed at the only thing it was for.

## 9. HOW TO RUN IT

### If you are running this on yourself

Do not answer the four questions cold. Self-report is the least reliable input
there is. Instead, gather evidence first:

1. **What you actually get paid for.** Pull the last ten invoices. Not what you wish they said.
2. **What people already call you about.** Search your inbox and messages for "can you help me with." The pattern in the requests is your real positioning, whether you chose it or not.
3. **What you say when you are tired.** How you describe the work at the end of a long day, to someone who is not a prospect. That version is usually the true one.
4. **The last five things you shipped.** What do they have in common? If the answer is "nothing," that is your score.

Then draft the four lines from that evidence and score them. Reacting to a
draft beats generating from a blank page.

### If you are running this for a client or a colleague

Draft the four lines **for** them before asking. People defend a draft more
honestly than they generate from nothing, and the corrections they make are
better data than their first answer would have been.

### Scoring discipline

Be adversarial. A generous score is a disservice, because the market does not
grade on a curve. If the honest read is 44, say 44. Justify every 5 with a
specific instance. An unjustified 5 becomes a 3.

### Cadence

Quarterly at most. Immediately on any pivot, new offer, or new audience.
Positioning moves on a slow clock. Weekly re-scoring is procrastination with a
rubric attached.

## 10. WORKED EXAMPLE

A regional commercial contractor, scored honestly.

```
UNIQUENESS CARD — Example Contracting — Q3

PROBLEM   : Overruns
AUDIENCE  : Tribal housing authorities
MESSAGE   : We finish federally funded housing projects on the original schedule.
REVENUE   : HUD-funded multi-unit builds

GATES     : G1 [P]  G2 [P]  G3 [P]  G4 [P]  G5 [P]  G6 [untested]

GRID
                 S    O    D    V    E   | Subtotal
PROBLEM          5    3    5    4    4   |   21/25
AUDIENCE         5    4    4    4    5   |   22/25
MESSAGE          4    4    5    5    3   |   21/25
REVENUE          4    4    4    3    4   |   19/25

WALL SCORE       : 83/100
DILUTION INDEX   : 6  (3 offers x 1 audience x 2 streams)
VERDICT          : BREACHING (provisional, G6 untested)

THE CONSTRAINT   : MESSAGE / Evidence (3). The on-schedule claim has no number
                   attached to it in public.
THE ONE MOVE     : Publish the completion-date record for the last 6 projects,
                   with dates. Owner: Ops. Due: 30 days.
NOT DOING        : Residential remodels. General commercial bids outside
                   federally funded work.
THIS WEEK        : Pull the contract dates and actual completion dates for the
                   last 6 jobs into one sheet.
```

Note what is happening here. The score is high not because the company is big,
but because it is narrow. "Overruns / tribal housing authorities" is a smaller
target than "construction / anyone building anything," and that is precisely
why it scores. The one move is not a marketing campaign. It is publishing a
number they already have.

## 12. ANTI-PATTERNS

1. **Scoring an aspiration.** Score what is true today. The future version scores itself in 90 days.
2. **Accepting two words.** "Business automation" is two words, which means two lenses. Push until it is one.
3. **Confusing narrow with small.** The one word is the entry point, not the ceiling. Everything else still gets sold, just later, and to people who already trust you.
4. **Running it and changing nothing.** A score with no deletion is a vanity metric. The NOT DOING list is the actual deliverable.
5. **Re-running it weekly.** See cadence above.
6. **Fixing the message when the problem word is broken.** Almost every fuzzy message is a downstream symptom of a category-level problem word. Fix the word first and the sentence usually writes itself.
